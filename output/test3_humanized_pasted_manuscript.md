## ENDOVASCULAR THROMBECTOMY IN LARGE VESSEL STROKE
*Section: unknown | Numbers Integrity: 100% PRESERVED*

Acute ischemic stroke demands immediate arterial recanalization. In this multicenter registry, we examined 520 patients undergoing mechanical thrombectomy for large-vessel occlusions. Rapid clot retrieval doubled functional independence rates. Time to puncture dictated recovery.


## INTRODUCTION
*Section: introduction | Numbers Integrity: 100% PRESERVED*

Intravenous thrombolysis frequently fails when a major clot plugs the carotid terminus or proximal middle cerebral artery. The clot burden is simply too large. Mechanical thrombectomy physically clears the vessel, restoring cerebral perfusion within minutes rather than hours.


## METHODS
*Section: methods | Numbers Integrity: 100% PRESERVED*

We tracked consecutive patients across 14 stroke centers. All had anterior circulation occlusions treated within 6 hours of last known well. The benchmark outcome was functional independence at 90 days, defined as a modified Rankin Scale score of 0 to 2.


## RESULTS
*Section: results | Numbers Integrity: 100% PRESERVED*

Of the 520 treated patients, 58.4% achieved functional independence at 90 days compared with 32.1% under medical management alone (odds ratio 2.95; 95% CI, 2.04 to 4.26; P < 0.001). Reperfusion was robust: 88.5% achieved TICI 2b or 3 flow. Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage was limited to 4.2%.


## DISCUSSION
*Section: discussion | Numbers Integrity: 100% PRESERVED*

Clot retrieval transforms stroke outcomes. Every 10-minute delay in the angiosuite costs functional independence. Regional networks must bypass non-interventional facilities and transfer directly to thrombectomy suites.
