## SGLT2 INHIBITION AND CARDIORENAL OUTCOMES IN TYPE 2 DIABETES
*Section: unknown | Numbers Guard: PASSED (0 Discrepancies)*

Diabetic kidney disease drives most dialysis cases worldwide. We tested whether SGLT2 inhibition alters that trajectory. In this multinational trial, 4,401 patients received either canagliflozin or placebo across a median follow-up of 2.6 years. Renal protection was pronounced.


## INTRODUCTION
*Section: introduction | Numbers Guard: PASSED (0 Discrepancies)*

Cardiorenal damage strikes diabetic patients hard. Even with maximum-dose ACE inhibitors, kidneys fail. We needed agents that tackle hemodynamics through distinct metabolic pathways.


## RESULTS
*Section: results | Numbers Guard: PASSED (0 Discrepancies)*

End-stage kidney disease dropped by 30% with canagliflozin (HR 0.70; 95% CI, 0.59 to 0.82; P < 0.001). The benefit came swiftly. Heart failure hospitalizations fell by 39% (HR 0.61; 95% CI, 0.47 to 0.80). Glycemic differences between arms narrowed over time, but the renal divergence widened.


## DISCUSSION
*Section: discussion | Numbers Guard: PASSED (0 Discrepancies)*

Canagliflozin shields both nephrons and myocardium. Waiting for overt proteinuria before starting therapy wastes critical nephron mass. Clinicians should start early.
